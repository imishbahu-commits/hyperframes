# USA Finance Short — “The Fed Just Changed Your Money”

## Voiceover script

The Federal Reserve just raised interest rates for the first time in more than three years—and your money may feel it before your next statement arrives.

The new federal-funds target is three-point-seven-five to four percent. Why? Inflation is still elevated, while spending, jobs, and economic growth remain resilient.

Here’s the wallet impact.

Variable-rate credit cards can become more expensive. Mortgage rates do not copy the Fed directly, but high Treasury yields can keep home loans under pressure. Savers may still find attractive yields in competitive savings accounts and short-term Treasuries. And expensive growth stocks can face tougher valuations when safe yields rise.

Three moves to consider: attack high-interest variable debt, compare your savings rate, and avoid panic-selling a diversified portfolio because of one headline.

The real story is not one rate hike. It’s how long rates stay high.

This is general information, not personalized financial advice.
